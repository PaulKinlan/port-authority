A port is not tied to a page. It can live in a worker, and what travels over it can be video, or the use of a key. These are about what that makes possible.

Block on a port: a synchronous call with no await

Ports are asynchronous, and a great deal of old code is not. Add a few bytes of shared memory and a worker can ask a question down a port and stop dead on that line until the answer is written, even when the answer is a person pressing a button. Shared memory needs a cross-origin isolated page, so this one has a room of its own.

Things that will bite you

  • A port you attach with addEventListener stays silent until you call port.start(). Setting onmessage starts it for you.
  • Messages posted before the other side is listening are not lost. They wait in the port. You can send a port and start talking into it straight away.
  • Nothing tells you when the other end has gone. A close event for ports has been specified, but the Chromium this was tested in does not fire it. Between pages on one origin, hold a Web Lock in the page and wait on it from the worker. Across origins, send a ping and treat silence as the answer.
  • A SharedWorker inside a cross-site iframe is partitioned by the top-level site, so a hidden hub iframe is a different hub on every site. A window of its own is first party and reaches the real one. Ports that have already been handed over are not affected.
  • A transferred port is gone from the sender. Post it twice and the second call throws.
  • A stream that is passed on through a middle context keeps flowing through it. If the middle goes away the reader stalls: no error, no end. Send streams down a direct port.
  • A port cannot be saved anywhere. IndexedDB, sessionStorage and history state all refuse it. To keep one across a reload, post it to a SharedWorker in pagehide and ask for it back. Create the worker with extendedLifetime: true, or it dies with its last page.
